Approach — 04 steps

How a fashion training becomes a research practice

01

Start from the person

Fashion taught me to design for a body, a season and an occasion — never for an abstract user. Interviews are the same instinct, formalised.

02

Turn notes into implications

A quote is not a finding. I code observations, cluster them, and force each cluster to produce something the interface must do differently.

03

Build it, don't mock it

Prototypes in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, so interactions can be tested at real speed with real keyboard behaviour.

04

Test, and say what broke

Every project lists the issues found and what changed. The failures are the part worth reading.

About

From garments to interfaces

I hold a Bachelor of Fashion Design from Iqra University, Islamabad, where alongside illustration, draping and collection development I studied computer science fundamentals, multimedia, psychology and communication. I have spent several years working as a freelance hair and makeup artist across commercial campaigns, editorials and fashion shows in Dubai.

What pulled me toward human–computer interaction was noticing how much of that work was really about attention: what a person looks at first, what they misread, what makes them hesitate. Those are interface questions. I am now formalising that instinct through UX research methods and front-end development.
